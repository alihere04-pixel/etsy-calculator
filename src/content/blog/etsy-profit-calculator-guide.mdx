Pricing on Etsy means covering fees, materials, shipping, and labour while still leaving profit. The Etsy Fee Calculator does the math for you — you enter your numbers, it subtracts every Etsy fee, and shows what actually lands in your pocket. This guide walks through the inputs, the outputs, and a full pricing example you can copy.

## What You Enter

The calculator asks for the facts of one order:

- **Product price:** what the buyer pays for the item.
- **Shipping charged to the buyer:** included in Etsy's fee base, so it must be in the calculation.
- **COGS:** your materials, packaging, and product cost.
- **Shipping cost paid by seller:** what you actually pay the carrier.
- **Quantity, country, Offsite Ads status:** these select the right fee rates for your market.
- **Tax-inclusive toggle (UK/EU):** for prices that already include VAT.
- **Gift wrap:** if you charge it, it joins the fee base too.

Nothing leaves your browser — the calculation runs locally with the current rate data for your country.

## What You Get Back

The output covers every fee and the numbers that actually matter for pricing:

- Listing, transaction, processing, regulatory and Offsite Ads fees
- Total fees and net revenue
- **Net profit and profit margin**
- **Break-even price**
- Effective fee rate (total fees as a share of the order)

Margin is calculated as net profit divided by net revenue, so it reflects the money you keep after Etsy, not a guess based on your sticker price.

## Step by Step: Pricing a Mug

Suppose you want to sell a mug in the US. Your costs: $8.00 for the mug and packaging, $4.50 to ship it. Total cost to you: $12.50. You plan to charge $25.00 with $5.00 shipping, so the order total Etsy sees is $30.00.

Fees on that $30 order:

- Listing fee: $0.20
- Transaction fee: 6.5% x $30 = $1.95
- Payment processing: 3% x $30 + $0.25 = $1.15
- Regulatory fee: none

**Total fees: $3.30.** Net revenue is $30.00 − $3.30 = $26.70. Subtract your $12.50 in costs and you keep **$14.20 net profit**, a margin of 53% on net revenue. That is a healthy listing.

## Reading the Break-Even Price

Break-even is the order total where net profit hits zero. With $12.50 in costs and US rates (9.5% variable fees plus $0.45 in fixed fees), you need an order total of about **$14.31** to cover everything. Below that you are losing money on every sale; above it, every dollar is income. Use the calculator's break-even number as your floor — never price below it, even for a promotion.

## Setting a Target Margin

Once break-even is known, price up from it. A common target for handmade sellers is 40–60% margin on net revenue after fees and COGS. If your break-even order total is $14.31 and you want a 50% margin on net revenue, you need net revenue roughly double your costs — meaning an order total near $29–$30. That is exactly how the $25 + $5 pricing above was validated. Adjust the target for your niche: high-return categories need more cushion.

## Offsite Ads Change the Math

If an order arrives via an Offsite Ad, Etsy adds 15% (or 12% if you qualify) of the order total. On the $30 mug order that is an extra $4.50 — total fees jump from $3.30 to $7.80 and profit drops from $14.20 to $9.70. If most of your traffic is ad-driven, price with the 15% scenario as your default, not as an exception.

## Common Pricing Mistakes

- Pricing from COGS alone and discovering the fee stack at payout time.
- Forgetting the listing fee ($0.20) on every sale.
- Leaving shipping charged out of the fee base — Etsy charges fees on it.
- Using a single country's rates when you sell internationally.
- Ignoring Offsite Ads on promoted orders.
- Treating sticker price as revenue instead of net revenue after fees.

## Tips That Protect Margin

- Run every price through the calculator before publishing, including sale prices.
- Check the effective fee rate: if it sits above 15%, your order total is probably too small.
- Re-run the numbers whenever Etsy updates rates (the data shows a last-updated date).
- Bundle low-cost items so the $0.20 listing and $0.25 processing fees spread over a bigger order.
- Review margin quarterly as COGS and shipping rates drift.

## Reverse the Thinking: Start From Profit

A stronger workflow is to decide the profit you need first. Want $10 net from each mug? Enter your costs and target, and let the calculator tell you the price that delivers it after all fees. When the required price feels too high for the market, the honest options are cheaper COGS, cheaper shipping, or a different product — not optimistic fee assumptions.

## A Note on Currency Markets

In the UK, EU, and Canada, the processing percentage is higher and a regulatory fee may apply; in India the flat ₹25 processing fee dominates small orders. The same pricing discipline applies — the calculator simply loads the right rates when you pick your country, so one workflow works for every market you sell in.

Use the break-even price as your floor and aim for a margin that pays you for your time. The calculator turns "I think this price works" into "I know this price works."
