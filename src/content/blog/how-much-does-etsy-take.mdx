Etsy takes a listing fee plus a 6.5% transaction fee plus a payment processing fee that varies by country, and in some markets a small regulatory fee as well. The exact amount depends on where your buyer is, what you charged for shipping, and whether the order came from an Offsite Ad. Here are real examples with real numbers for every Etsy market.

## The Quick Answer

For every order, Etsy's take is:

**listing fee + 6.5% x order total + (processing% x order total + fixed fee) + regulatory fee (if any)**

The order total means item price plus shipping charged to the buyer plus gift wrap. On a typical US order around $55, Etsy keeps roughly $5.68 — close to 10% of the sale. Markets with higher processing percentages or regulatory fees come out slightly higher.

## Example 1: US Sale of $55

A $50 item with $5 shipping to a US buyer:

- Listing fee: $0.20
- Transaction fee: 6.5% x $55 = $3.58
- Payment processing: 3% x $55 + $0.25 = $1.90
- Regulatory fee: none

**Etsy takes about $5.68 of the $55.** That leaves $49.32 before COGS and shipping costs. Your effective fee rate is 10.3%.

## Example 2: UK Sale of £55

The same order shape in the UK:

- Listing fee: £0.20
- Transaction fee: 6.5% x £55 = £3.58
- Payment processing: 4% x £55 + £0.20 = £2.40
- Regulatory operating fee: 0.48% x £55 = £0.26

**Etsy takes about £6.44.** The higher processing percentage plus the regulatory fee make the UK about 11.7% effective — noticeably more than the US on the same order size.

## Example 3: EU Sale of €55

- Listing fee: €0.20
- Transaction fee: 6.5% x €55 = €3.58
- Payment processing: 4% x €55 + €0.30 = €2.50

**Etsy takes about €6.28 before the country regulatory fee.** The EU regulatory fee varies by country and is marked unverified in our rate data, so add your local rate on top if it applies. Always verify your country's specific charge.

## Example 4: Canada Sale of C$55

- Listing fee: C$0.20
- Transaction fee: 6.5% x C$55 = C$3.58
- Payment processing: 3% x C$55 + C$0.25 = C$1.90
- Regulatory operating fee: 0.50% x C$55 = C$0.28

**Etsy takes about C$5.95.** Canada's regulatory fee is the highest published percentage of the major markets, so it is worth building into your pricing rather than discovering it at payout time.

## Example 5: Australia Sale of A$55

- Listing fee: A$0.20
- Transaction fee: 6.5% x A$55 = A$3.58
- Payment processing: 3% x A$55 + A$0.25 = A$1.90
- Regulatory fee: none

**Etsy takes about A$5.68** — identical structure to the US, and about 10.3% effective.

## Example 6: India — Two Order Sizes

India shows how the flat fees dominate small orders. On a ₹55 order:

- Listing fee: ₹0.20
- Transaction fee: 6.5% x ₹55 = ₹3.58
- Payment processing: 5% x ₹55 + ₹25 = ₹27.75
- Regulatory operating fee: 0.05% x ₹55 = ₹0.03

**Etsy takes ₹31.55 of ₹55 — a 57% effective rate.** Now on a ₹1,000 order:

- Listing fee: ₹0.20
- Transaction fee: ₹65.00
- Payment processing: ₹50 + ₹25 = ₹75.00
- Regulatory fee: ₹0.50

**Etsy takes ₹140.70 — about 14.1%.** The flat ₹25 processing fee barely moves while the percentages scale, which is why small-ticket pricing rarely works in the Indian market.

## What About Offsite Ads?

If the order came through an Etsy Offsite Ad, add 15% of the order total (or 12% for eligible sellers) on top. The $55 US order goes from $5.68 in fees to about $13.93 — a 25% effective rate. Offsite Ads fees do not replace any other fee; they stack on top of the full list above.

## Cheap Items vs Expensive Items

Because the listing fee and the processing fixed fee are flat, cheap orders pay a much higher percentage:

- **$10 order (US):** $0.20 + $0.65 + $0.55 = **$1.40 fees — 14.0%**
- **$200 order (US):** $0.20 + $13.00 + $6.25 = **$19.45 fees — 9.7%**

Same fee rules, very different bite. This is why a $5 handmade item can be unprofitable while a $100 item with the same effort pays well.

## Common Mistakes

- Calculating fees on the item price and forgetting the shipping charged to the buyer.
- Using one country's processing rate for all your sales.
- Ignoring the regulatory fee because the percentage looks small.
- Pricing small items without noticing how the fixed fees stack (see the India example).
- Not accounting for Offsite Ads on promoted orders — 15% changes everything.
- Forgetting the 2.5% currency conversion fee when listing and payout currencies differ.

## Tips

- Run every candidate price through the calculator for your specific market before publishing.
- Raise the order total (bundles, free-shipping thresholds built into price) to dilute the flat fees.
- Check your effective fee rate — total fees divided by order total — monthly, not just per sale.
- Deactivate listings that never sell so the $0.20 renewal stops.
- Keep listing and payout currency the same to avoid the conversion fee.

## Where Etsy Keeps the Most

Across these examples, the effective rate on a mid-size order runs from about 10% (US, AU) to roughly 12% (UK, EU, Canada) and far higher on very small orders. Offsite Ads orders can exceed 25%. Knowing your number is the difference between guessing and pricing with confidence.
