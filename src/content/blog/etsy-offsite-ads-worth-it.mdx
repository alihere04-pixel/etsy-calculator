Etsy Offsite Ads sounds great on paper: Etsy advertises your listings on Google, Instagram, Pinterest, and other channels, and you only pay when a sale comes through. But is it actually worth it? This guide breaks down the 15% vs 12% rate, the $10,000 annual threshold, and real examples so you can decide.

## What Are Etsy Offsite Ads?

Offsite Ads are Etsy's program that promotes your listings on external platforms like Google search, Facebook, Instagram, Pinterest, and Bing. When a buyer clicks an ad and buys one of your items, Etsy charges you an Offsite Ads fee on that order.

The fee is charged in addition to the normal fees: listing fee, transaction fee, and payment processing. Offsite Ads does not replace those.

Source: Etsy Fees & Payments Policy.

## The Two Rates: 15% and 12%

- **15%** applies if your shop has made less than $10,000 in sales over the past 12 months. You can also opt out of the program entirely if you are under that threshold.
- **12%** applies if your shop has made $10,000 or more in the past 12 months. Participation is mandatory once you cross that mark.

In both cases the fee is capped per order, and in both cases it applies on top of the standard fee stack. Check the calculator to see what that means for a specific order. Source: Etsy Fees & Payments Policy.

## Real Example: $50 US Sale

Take a $50 item with $5 shipping charged to the buyer:

- Transaction fee (6.5%): $3.58
- Payment processing (3% + $0.25): $1.90
- Listing fee: $0.20
- Offsite Ads at 15% on the $55 order total: about $8.25

Total platform fees before Offsite Ads: about $5.68. With Offsite Ads on a 15% order: about $13.93. That is a big jump, so every Offsite Ads order needs to still leave you profit after COGS and shipping.

## When Offsite Ads Are Worth It

- You have healthy margins (ideally 40%+ after fees, COGS, and shipping) so a 15% fee does not wipe out profit.
- You use it to reach buyers you would not otherwise reach, especially for items with strong search appeal outside Etsy.
- You track the fee per order. Treat Offsite Ads orders like a separate channel with its own unit economics.

## When They Are Not Worth It

- You sell low-margin items where 15% on the order total pushes you below break-even.
- You already convert well inside Etsy search and the extra channel adds cost without enough incremental volume.
- You are under the $10,000 threshold and can opt out: if the numbers do not work, opting out is a legitimate choice.

## Pros and Cons

Pros: you only pay on a sale, you reach audiences outside Etsy, and the alternative (running your own ads) would cost more time and setup. Cons: the rate eats margin, you cannot opt out once you cross $10,000 a year, and it stacks with the transaction and processing fees that still apply.

## How the Fee Stacks on Top of Normal Fees

Offsite Ads is not the only fee on a sale. The listing fee, the 6.5% transaction fee, and payment processing all still apply. On a US order of $55 that is about $5.68 before Offsite Ads. At 15% for Offsite Ads on the same order the total jumps to about $13.93. Every Offsite Ads order therefore needs a price that absorbs the full stack, not just the ad fee.

## A Simple Test Before You Opt In

Ask one question: if a sale came from an ad, would I still make money at these numbers? Take your current margin after all fees and subtract 15% of the order total. If the answer stays positive, Offsite Ads can be worth it. If it goes negative, either raise your price or stay opted out while you are under the $10,000 threshold.

## What Sellers Often Miss

The fee is charged on the order total, which includes shipping and gift wrap you charge the buyer. That makes the effective rate higher than the headline 15% of your product price. On a $30 item with $5 shipping, 15% of $35 is $5.25, not $4.50. Always calculate on the order total.

## The Bottom Line

Offsite Ads can be worth it for healthy-margin shops that understand the true per-order cost. For thin-margin sellers, it often turns a sale into a loss.
