Every item you list on Etsy costs $0.20. That is the listing fee, and it is charged whether the item sells or not. This guide explains exactly when it is charged, how auto-renewal works, and how to keep it from eating your margin. Source: Etsy Fees & Payments Policy.

## What the Listing Fee Is

The listing fee is $0.20 per item listed for sale. It is a flat fee, not a percentage, so it does not scale with your price. A $5 item and a $500 item both cost $0.20 to list.

You are charged the fee when you publish the listing, and Etsy does not refund it if the item does not sell.

## When You Are Charged

- When you publish a new listing: $0.20
- When the listing auto-renews every 4 months: $0.20
- When the listing auto-renews because it sold (each sale renews the listing): $0.20

If a listing sells and auto-renews, the new $0.20 fee is added to the next sale's fees. So a popular item effectively costs $0.20 per sale in listing fees.

## Auto-Renewal in Practice

Etsy listings expire after 4 months. You can choose auto-renew, which charges $0.20 to relist, or manual renewal, where you pay $0.20 yourself each time. If you sell out of an item and enable auto-renew, the next sale of that item starts a new 4-month period and charges another $0.20.

Example: a mug shop with 50 active listings that auto-renew every 4 months pays 50 x $0.20 = $10 per renewal cycle, roughly $2.50 per month in listing fees alone, even if nothing sells.

## Bulk Listings and Variations

Each listing is charged $0.20 regardless of how many items or variations (size, color) it has. One listing with 10 variations is one $0.20 charge, not ten. Bulk uploading does not change the per-listing fee; it just groups the charges together.

Example: 100 listings cost $20. 100 variations inside 10 listings cost $2. Consolidating variations into fewer listings is the easiest way to cut listing fees.

## How to Minimize Listing Fees

- Combine variations (color, size) into a single listing.
- Remove stale listings that have not sold in months; each active listing that renews costs $0.20.
- Price so the listing fee is a small percentage of the item price. On a $25 item, $0.20 is 0.8%. On a $5 item, it is 4%, which hurts.
- Use auto-renew only for items that actually sell.

## Listing Fees vs Transaction Fees

The listing fee is separate from the 6.5% transaction fee. A sale pays both: $0.20 listing plus a percentage. Because the listing fee is flat, it is proportionally bigger on cheap items. A $10 item pays 2% in listing fees alone; a $100 item pays 0.2%.

## What Happens on Renewal

A listing stays active for 4 months or until it sells. If it sells, it auto-renews for another 4 months or the next sale. Each renewal costs $0.20. If you have 200 active listings that cycle twice a year on average, that is 200 x 2 x $0.20 = $80 a year in renewals alone, before any sales.

## Digital Downloads and Variations

Digital items also cost $0.20 to list. Variations of one item (different sizes or colors) live inside a single listing, so combining them saves money. Splitting them into separate listings multiplies the listing fee.

## Listing Fees in Your Monthly Cost

Add listing fees to your COGS and shipping when you price. If you list 30 items a month and sell 10, your unsold 20 still renewed at $0.20 each if you used auto-renew. That is $4 of pure cost. Deactivating slow listings stops that spend.

## Real Example

A shop lists a $30 item with $5 shipping. The listing fee is $0.20, transaction fee 6.5% is about $2.28, and US payment processing 3% + $0.25 is about $1.30. Total fixed fees about $3.78. The $0.20 listing fee is about 5% of that total. It is small, but it is never zero, and it adds up across a large catalog.

## Quick Recap

Listing fee is $0.20 per listing, charged on publish and on each auto-renewal or sale-driven renewal. It does not scale with price, so it hurts more on cheap items. Consolidate variations, prune stale listings, and price so $0.20 is a small share of the item price. That alone keeps the fee from being a silent tax on your margin.
