Profit margin tells you how much of each sale you actually keep. On Etsy it is easy to overestimate because fees stack: listing, transaction, processing, regulatory, and sometimes Offsite Ads. This guide gives you the formula, a full worked example, and the mistakes that make sellers think they are profitable when they are not. Source: Etsy Fees & Payments Policy.

## The Formula

Profit margin = (Net profit / Net revenue) x 100

Where:

- Net revenue = (price + shipping charged) - total platform fees
- Net profit = net revenue - (COGS + shipping cost paid by you)

Total platform fees = listing fee + transaction fee + payment processing + regulatory fee + Offsite Ads fee (if the sale came from one).

## Step-by-Step Example (US)

You sell an item for $40, charge the buyer $5 shipping, COGS is $12, and you pay $4 to ship it. No Offsite Ads.

1. Order total: $40 + $5 = $45.
2. Listing fee: $0.20.
3. Transaction fee: 6.5% of $45 = $2.93 (rounded: $2.925).
4. Payment processing: 3% of $45 + $0.25 = $1.35 + $0.25 = $1.60.
5. Total fees: 0.20 + 2.93 + 1.60 = $4.73.
6. Net revenue: 45 - 4.73 = $40.27.
7. Net profit: 40.27 - (12 + 4) = $24.27.
8. Margin: 24.27 / 40.27 x 100 = about 60.3%.

That 60% looks strong. But change two inputs and it collapses. If COGS rises to $20, profit is 40.27 - 24 = $16.27, margin 40.4%. If the sale also carries a 15% Offsite Ads fee on the $45 total ($6.75), fees become $11.48, net revenue $33.52, and with $20 COGS profit is $9.52, margin 28.4%.

## Margin vs Markup

A 50% markup is not a 50% margin. If you buy for $20 and sell for $30, markup is 50% but margin is (10 / 30) x 100 = 33.3%. On Etsy, always talk in margin after fees. This is why sellers who say they have 50% margins sometimes have far less once fees are counted.

## Break-Even Price

Your break-even price is the price at which net profit is zero. Below it you lose money. The Etsy Fee Calculator returns it directly. As a rule of thumb, your price should be at least COGS plus shipping plus fees plus the margin you want. If your landed cost is $16 and fees take about 10% of the order, a $25 price leaves about $6.50 margin, roughly 30%.

## Volume Helps Margin

Fixed fees amortize as volume rises. At 10 orders a month, a $25/month indirect cost (apps, ads tools) is $2.50 per order. At 100 orders it is $0.25. That is why a shop can be unprofitable at low volume and profitable at higher volume on the same item.

## Common Mistakes

- Forgetting the listing fee ($0.20) on every sale.
- Not counting the 6.5% transaction fee on shipping charged to the buyer.
- Treating Offsite Ads as rare; crossing the $10,000 annual threshold makes 12% mandatory.
- Ignoring the regulatory fee in the UK (0.48%), CA (0.50%), and IN (0.05%).
- Calculating margin on price instead of net revenue. Always divide by net revenue after fees.

## How to Improve Your Margin

- Raise prices so the fixed $0.25 processing and $0.20 listing fees are a smaller share.
- Cut COGS: even $2 saved per unit is $2 more profit.
- Offer free shipping by building the cost into the price, so you keep control of the shipping line.
- Watch Offsite Ads orders separately and price so the 15% fee does not turn them into losses.
- Review slow listings: each one still costs $0.20 on renewal.

## Quick Recap

Profit margin = (net profit / net revenue) x 100, where net revenue is the order total minus all platform fees. Subtract COGS and shipping you paid from net revenue to get profit. Count the listing fee, the 6.5% transaction fee, payment processing, any regulatory fee, and Offsite Ads when they apply. A sale that looks like 50% margin after COGS alone can be 25-30% once every fee is counted. Run the exact numbers in the Etsy Fee Calculator before you commit to a price.
